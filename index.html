Coordinates are in metres, radius in millimetres. Any cell takes an expression: + − * / ( ) with R = λ at the design frequency, I = inch, F = foot, e.g. R/4 or 33*F. Wires connect where their end points coincide exactly. Seg: -1 tapers both ends (recommended), 0 automatic even, a positive number fixes the count.

Wires

Add a tube element (telescoping sections)

Builds a straight element along Y from tube sections, centre outward: each section as half-length in metres × diameter in mm, for example 1.2x32, 1.2x25, 1.5x20, 1.55x16. The centre section becomes one wire across the middle (feed it at its centre, c), the others one wire per side. Sommerwire calculates the steps in diameter directly; see Help, Tube elements.

Sources

Position w1c = wire 1 centre, w1b = begin, w1e = end. Voltage sources. Balun n:1 puts an ideal transformer at the feed point (4 for a 4:1 unun, 49 for an EFHW's 49:1): the impedance and SWR are then those the feed line sees.

Loads

Series L/C, a parallel L/C trap, or a fixed R + jX. Leave L or C at 0 to leave it out. Q is the coil's, Q C the capacitor's (empty = lossless); Cp is the coil's own self-capacitance, across the coil.

Transmission lines

A line between two feed points, connected across the gap at each end like NEC's TL card. It does not radiate or couple to the wires, so it suits coax. Leave Length empty for the straight distance between the ends; the velocity factor makes the line electrically longer. Loss is the matched loss in dB per 100 m from the cable's data sheet; with "at MHz" it is scaled to other frequencies with √f, without it the same loss is used at every frequency. Reversed swaps the two conductors at the far end. A source on a line end drives the line and the wire gap together.

Buildings

A house beside the antenna. X and Y are the centre of the footprint, Length runs along the ridge, Rotation turns the ridge away from the X axis. A metal roof is solved as a wire grid (mesh about λ/10 at the highest frequency or finer: 1 m suits HF); finer meshes are slower. Walls are shown for reference only: stone, brick and wood are not conductors, and a wire solver cannot model them. Metal gutters run along both eaves (not bonded to the roof); downpipes run from the gutter ends into the ground, each with an earth resistance (typically tens to hundreds of ohms at HF; 0 = perfect). If your downpipes end in plastic drains, choose None. Wiring or a steel frame can be drawn as wires. Add height on the Calculate tab lifts buildings too.

Conditions

With Real ground, the impedance is solved with Sommerfeld integrals, as NEC-2 does: exact for a flat homogeneous ground, so wires a few centimetres above the soil (radials, low dipoles, feed lines) come out right, ground loss included. "Complex image" is a faster approximation that underestimates ground loss closer to the soil than about 0.2 λ. "Perfect mirror" reproduces MININEC and MMANA-GAL. If any wire touches the ground, the whole impedance is solved over the perfect mirror; buried wires are not supported. The far field uses Fresnel reflection. Tapered segments run from λ/(SC·DM1) at wire ends to λ/DM2 in the middle.

    What the initial release got wrong

    • Joeri found and reproduced a gain error: a 20 m-band dipole 1 m above average soil showed 10.3 dBi and a pattern implying 1.82 times the input power when the app combined mirror currents with real-ground reflection. Joeri documented the problem and submitted a patch to hide the affected patterns and gain. Koen closed the PR without merging it and applied a different fix by hand in v1.1.1: the app marks the gain approximate (≈) and caps integrated radiated power at the input power. Joeri’s report and patch (upstream PR #1) · Koen’s confirmation and credit · Koen’s v1.1.1 fix.
    • After a model edit, sweep charts and band summaries could still show the previous antenna’s results. Koen merged Joeri’s fix to clear them and discard late results from the old sweep. Upstream PR #2.
    • A wire extending below the ground plane could produce numbers after a warning, even though the solver cannot model buried wire. Koen merged Joeri’s check that stops the calculation and identifies the wire when it extends beyond the contact tolerance. Upstream PR #6.
    • The optimizer could favour a trial that touched ground because switching to the mirror made gain look better. Following Joeri’s report, Koen excluded those trials when a search starts with a full ground solve. Upstream issue #3 · Optimizer fix.

    Model limits and validation

    • Joeri compared the fast complex-image approximation with Sommerfeld near the soil and reviewed automatic wire coarsening. Koen independently revised the height warning, displayed effective segment counts and explained how to check a finer mesh. Upstream response to PRs #4, #5, #7 and #8.
    • Joeri found that the first DX Commander comparison used copper in Sommerwire and lossless wire in NEC-2. Koen reran it with matched materials and archived the runs. The remaining difference at the hub limits what that comparison can establish. Corrected comparison · Archived evidence.

    Sommerwire is an independent thin-wire moment-method solver in the MININEC style (pulse currents at nodes, reduced kernel, image ground). It reads and writes the plain-text .maa layout used by MMANA-GAL so you can bring models across, but it is not MMANA-GAL and its numbers can differ slightly. The optimizer is a bounded Nelder–Mead search, restarted with smaller steps.
